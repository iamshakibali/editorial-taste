01 — How it works

Three parts, no tools.

Every Kestrel is a battery, a controller in the tiller, and a sealed motor in the leg. Each part comes off in under a minute.

See the models
  1. 01

    Battery

    A 1.4 kWh pack that floats, with a handle and a charge gauge on top. A full charge from a household socket takes 4 hours 20 minutes.

  2. 02

    Controller

    A twist tiller with a screen that shows range left at your current speed, not just percent. It pairs with your phone for trip logs.

  3. 03

    Motor

    A brushless motor sealed in the lower leg, cooled by the water around it. No gearbox oil, no impeller, no annual service.

02 — Range

41 km

On one battery at 5 knots, measured on Lake Bled with a 4.2 m dinghy and two people aboard.

06 — Questions

Before you switch.

Will it fit my boat?

Kestrel mounts on any standard outboard bracket or transom from 30 to 70 mm thick. Measure from the top of the transom to the bottom of the hull: under 43 cm is short shaft, over is long.

Can I leave it in salt water?

Yes. The leg is anodised aluminium with a zinc anode. Tilt it out of the water when moored for more than a week, and rinse it with fresh water a few times a season.

How long does the battery last?

The cells are rated for 1,500 full cycles to 80% capacity. Most owners use around 60 cycles a year, so the warranty covers 5 years or 800 cycles, whichever comes first.

Do I need a licence?

That depends on your country. In most of Europe, electric motors under 15 kW need no licence on inland and coastal waters. Check with your local authority before you buy.

Where can I get it serviced?

There is very little to service. For repairs, we have 46 partner yards across Europe, and we collect from anywhere else within 5 working days.

Can I try one first?

Yes. Book a free test ride at a partner yard below. Rides take 40 minutes, and you can bring your own boat.
